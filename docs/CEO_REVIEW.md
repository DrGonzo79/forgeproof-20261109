# CEO review

## Challenge

The original framing risks selling “authenticity theater.” A maker can stage photos, a tag can move, and neither blockchain nor a score proves a human made the object. The report also overweights a 5.7M-seller TAM when the viable customer is a small fraction selling $200+ pieces. Zero solution-search volume means the $19 plan is not a distribution strategy. Verisart can copy features; regulation does not pull the opening jewelry segment.

The credible business is not “proof for every Etsy item.” It is an operational standard sold through trusted guilds, with measurable evidence assembly and buyer continuity. Even then, guild reviewers may avoid liability and makers may reject capture friction.

## Decisions made

1. Changed the buyer from individual seller to jewelry guild and the opening offer to a paid assisted pilot.
2. Replaced “verified handmade” language with “verified process record” and a prominent evidence-only disclaimer.
3. Made the prototype score completeness, not authenticity, and exposed every underlying check.
4. Deferred AI, blockchain and real NFC binding; none is needed to validate behavior.
5. Added stop thresholds, reviewer-time economics and a 30/60/90 validation sequence.
6. Kept the FastAPI domain operation deterministic and shared the demo fixture/rule vocabulary across both implementations.

## GTM and economics verdict

Proceed only as a concierge validation. Three written paid-pilot acceptances are stronger evidence than hundreds of free waitlist emails. If reviewer labor exceeds the subscription margin, price per reviewed record or stop. If members do not create a second passport, the workflow is a novelty.

## Architecture and failure verdict

The demo is proportionate and honest. Production must treat public/private field separation, tenant authorization, append-only events, consent withdrawal, tag cloning, upload idempotency and observability as launch gates. Do not deploy the API or store real evidence until those controls exist.

## Final verdict

**Conditional build.** The pain deserves a test; the standalone SaaS thesis does not yet deserve a platform. Sell and operate one guild pilot first.
